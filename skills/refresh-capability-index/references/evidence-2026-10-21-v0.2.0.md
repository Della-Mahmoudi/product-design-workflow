# Evidence summary: 0.2.0 release evaluation (2026-10-21)

Maintenance only. Normal routing does not load this file. It holds findings and limits only; case inputs, rubrics, oracles and raw answers are not shipped.

## Findings

Evidence level: output quality tested on synthetic, supplied-policy fixtures (no tools, no installed Skills).

- **Policy comparison.** Two samples per policy under the default model:
  - 0.1.3 passed 25 and the 0.2.0 candidate 24 of 28 calibration cases; both passed 3/3 held-out cases.
  - No hard failures.
  - No measured uplift. Two samples cannot establish superiority or regression.
- **Most frequent miss.** Plans did not separate what documentation says from what the current session can call: 0.1.3 missed it in 1 of 2 runs, the candidate in 2 of 2. A routing-policy sentence was added as an engineering clarification, not a proven causal fix.
- **Model profiles** (candidate policy, 1–2 samples each):
  - No general winner.
  - Sonnet met the preregistered bar only on that documentation-vs-availability criterion (2/2 against 0/2 for the default). This is maintenance evidence only, with no routing rule.
  - Fable, one sample: no evidence of benefit.
  - Inherit and opus carried the same trace-recorded model label. Requested alias, trace label and serving or billing identity remain separate; none is verified identity, version or price.
- **Corrections, recorded append-only.**
  - The data case ran in 10 calibration runs, not 12.
  - One grader summary sentence said 34/40 while its case results total 35/40.
  - An operator adjudication changed one PASS to FAIL: a second question moved into the recommendation field is still a second question. This gives 34/40 for that grader, and sonnet 12/14 in both samples.
- **Release regression of the final policy** (one run, three tasks):
  - The SVG-availability and Persian CTA tasks passed.
  - The documented-tool task is recorded as FAIL. Its answer had three sentences, against a two-sentence cutoff that was in the evaluator's rubric only; the candidate was asked only for "a short source-linked answer". Its content and no-probe checks passed, and the result does not show an instruction-following failure.
  - The documentation-vs-availability behavior appeared in two tasks.
  - No repair run.
- **Route smoke checks:**
  - Exact data reproduced by Python.
  - A local PNG rendered and its contrast computed.
  - An official doc fetched from the web.
  - Chrome viewing of one public page (text and screenshot).
  - A fresh worker executed one self-contained packet correctly.
  - The built-in browser check was unexecuted because the browser was disconnected.

## Limits

- The fixtures are synthetic and the policy was supplied in the prompt. These tests do not show installed Skill lookup, triggering, reference loading or real tool success. Nor do they show productivity on real team work.
- The shared answer format asks for route fields, which cues route composition under every policy tested.
- Workers saw host Skill listings and a memory index, so isolation is not proven. A tool-use count alone shows neither leakage nor isolation.
- Full subagent traces were not exported. Independent replay covers the retained prompts, outputs, checks and hashes, not complete source-trace reproduction.
- One regression cutoff (two sentences) was evaluator-only. Future test bundles must state every hard output limit to the candidate.
- Untested:
  - Figma reads and writes
  - Docs and Drive creation
  - Artifact publishing
  - media generation and quality
  - native writing quality
  - interactive browser behavior
  - account-level Skill activation
- Fixture flaws to fix in the next bundle:
  - an undefined "admin" term in the permission cases
  - a routing case and a data case sharing one scenario in the same bundle
